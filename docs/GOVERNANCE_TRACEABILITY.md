# Governance & Standards Traceability — v0.3.0

This document is a software-design traceability register, not a certification statement.

## Product architecture rule

Base role + department scope + supplemental capabilities + active assignments determines:

- navigation and landing workspace;
- visible datasets;
- permitted actions;
- notifications and help context;
- backend authorization/RLS.

UI hiding alone is never considered authorization.

## ISO 7101:2023 design alignment

The architecture is intended to support: leadership accountability, risk-based management, patient/workforce safety, controlled documented processes, measurable performance and continual improvement. Implementation evidence will be generated by audit history, workflow timestamps, ownership, status transitions, indicators and controlled configuration.

## JCI Hospital Standards, 8th Edition design alignment

The product is designed to make patient-safety and quality workflows observable and auditable, with special attention to event reporting, infection prevention and control, healthcare technology/cybersecurity, role-based access and evidence of completed actions. Formal JCI compliance can only be assessed against the licensed standards and the hospital's actual implementation/evidence.

## WHO IPC design alignment

The IPC domain will be built around programme governance, evidence-based guidelines, education/training, HAI surveillance, multimodal implementation, monitoring/feedback and structured improvement work. WHO IPCAF-compatible evidence should be derivable rather than entered twice.

## NHS IPC design alignment

Standard infection-control precautions and risk-based transmission prevention will be treated as operational controls/tasks, not merely as static reference documents.

## Greek regulatory design alignment

The software will support management accountability for HAI prevention/control, Infection Control Committee governance, internal IPC regulation/documentation, surveillance and indicator reporting. Greek national requirements prevail when they differ from external guidance.

## Release gate

Before a workflow is considered production-ready, verify:

1. role and scope visibility;
2. create/edit/complete/approve/delete/export/assign/manage rules;
3. equivalent backend/RLS enforcement;
4. immutable or append-only history where clinical/governance evidence requires it;
5. required timestamps, responsible actor and organization/department context;
6. indicators derivable from structured records;
7. notifications respect least privilege;
8. help content is role- and context-specific;
9. demo data are isolated from production;
10. legal/standards mapping has been reviewed for the released workflow.

## v0.5.0 — Clinical Surveillance Core

The clinical model now separates canonical domain records instead of storing the patient pathway as one mutable payload. Assessments, microbiology, antimicrobial therapy, isolation, reassessment and outcome remain relationally linked to the surveillance case and patient.

### Safety and governance controls added

- Laboratory samples and microbiology results are canonical laboratory records. Surveillance consumes them rather than duplicating them.
- Antimicrobial therapy is a canonical medication/stewardship record intended to be managed by the Pharmacy domain.
- Isolation is represented as an episode with start, review due date, end and responsible actors.
- Reassessments are append-oriented clinical evidence; an outcome is recorded separately from ongoing reviews.
- Clinical RLS no longer treats generic organization membership as sufficient to read the full patient record.
- Department-manager visibility requires department scope.
- Domain write rules are separated: laboratory writes laboratory data; pharmacy writes antimicrobial therapy; infection-control roles manage IPC clinical actions.
- Clinical audit records store metadata references rather than copying full clinical payloads.

### EL/EN release rule

Visible clinical terminology, statuses, actions, empty states and date presentation must resolve through the common language layer. New clinical components are not permitted to introduce hard-coded Greek or English UI labels.

This traceability register supports software design review. It is not a declaration of ISO certification, JCI accreditation or legal compliance by itself.
